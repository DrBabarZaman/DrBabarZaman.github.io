Statistical process control

Understanding variation. Detecting meaningful change.

The foundations

What is statistical process control?

Statistical process control (SPC) uses statistical methods to monitor a process over time. A baseline describes its usual behaviour; new observations are compared with that baseline to identify changes that warrant investigation. Classical control-chart methods include Shewhart, CUSUM and EWMA charts, with multivariate methods monitoring several characteristics together.

From methods to practice

Real-world applications

Examples of how these methods can be used in practice.

Manufacturing quality

Monitor dimensions, fill weights or defect counts and investigate changes in production.

Water quality

Track several water-quality measurements together to detect changes in a monitored process.

Energy and engineering

Monitor turbine and other equipment measurements for changes in operating behaviour.

My research

Research focus

My research develops mixed and adaptive memory control charts for process location and dispersion, robust score functions, and multivariate monitoring methods. The articles below include applications in semiconductor wafers, glass production, water quality and wind turbines.
